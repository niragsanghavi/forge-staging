How Forge works

Ten minutes and you'll know everything — how points actually work, what counts as a workout, and the one mistake nearly everyone makes in week one. There's a short quiz at the end.

The idea, in one paragraph

You log the workouts you actually did. Your group sees it. Points and streaks are calculated for you and your team, and a leaderboard shows how everyone's doing. That's the entire app.

It works for one reason: somebody notices when you stop. Every fitness app you've abandoned let you quit quietly. Here, a team streak dies if you skip — and it dies for people whose names you know.

Logging a workout

Open the app, tap the workout you did, done. Two taps. You can log for today, or any of the last 7 days if you forgot.

You can't log the future. And you can't go back further than about a week — otherwise "I definitely worked out on the 3rd" becomes a monthly negotiation.

What counts as a workout?

Forge is deliberately generous here. If you deliberately moved your body for a reasonable stretch of time, it counts.

Counts

  • Gym, weights, any lifting
  • Run, walk, cycle, swim
  • Cricket, football, padel, badminton, tennis, pickleball
  • Yoga, pilates, stretching, physio
  • Home workout, HIIT, skipping
  • A long deliberate walk
  • Dance, Zumba, martial arts

Doesn't count

  • Walking to the car
  • Housework and shopping
  • Standing at a wedding for six hours
  • A workout you're going to do tonight
  • Someone else's workout

Nobody audits you. The honour system is the whole design — cheating a fitness app is just an elaborate way of lying to yourself, and your group can see the feed anyway.

The part everyone gets wrong

Points are per day, not per workout

5

You get 5 points for a day you logged. Not 5 per workout. Gym in the morning and cricket in the evening is a great day — and it's still 5 points.

Log both anyway. The feed shows them, your stats count them, and the variety is genuinely good for you. But for scoring, the question is only ever: did you show up that day?

This is the single most common misunderstanding. Forge rewards showing up often, not doing more in one go. Six days of one workout beats two days of three.

Streaks — the two kinds

Your personal streak 🔥

Days in a row that you logged. It's on your home screen and it's pure pride — it earns no points. It exists to make you not want to break it, which turns out to be worth more than points.

Your team streak

This one does pay. Each day, if enough of your team logs, the team streak grows — and it pays more the longer it runs, up to 3 points a day.

"Enough" is a bit over half your team, rounded up. In a team of 5, three of you need to log that day. You don't need everyone — but you do need each other.

If you log and your team doesn't reach the number, you keep your 5 points. You're never punished for someone else's rest day. You just don't get the bonus.

Months and seasons

Forge runs in months. On the 1st, everything resets — points back to zero, leaderboard blank, everyone level again. Your history is kept, but the competition is fresh.

This is deliberate. A monthly reset means a terrible month never ruins the whole year, and someone joining on the 20th is only three weeks behind, not a lost cause.

Small things worth knowing

Your PIN. Four digits, set once. It stops someone else picking your name and logging as you. Set it the moment you join — before the group code gets shared around.

Apple Health and Health Connect. On the phone app you can let Forge see workouts your phone already recorded. It suggests them; you confirm with one tap. Your health data never leaves your phone.

Notifications. Forge sends one: a Monday-morning note that last week's recap is ready. It will never nag you to exercise. You already know you should.

Made a mistake? Tap the day on your calendar and remove the entry. Wrong name on your logs, or joined twice? Ask your group admin — they can fix it.
